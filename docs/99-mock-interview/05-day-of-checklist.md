# Day-of checklist

The interview is sometime in the next 1–2 weeks. This is what to do **the day before** and **the morning of**. Specific, no fluff.

---

## 24 hours before

### Stop building. Start consolidating.

- [ ] **Read the one-page summary** below. Don't open the repo to learn new things — just refresh what you already know.
- [ ] **Re-read your pitch** in [`docs/00-interview-prep/03-pitch-and-bridges.md`](../00-interview-prep/03-pitch-and-bridges.md). Out loud. Once.
- [ ] **Re-read the ⭐⭐⭐ questions** in [`01-likely-questions.md`](01-likely-questions.md). Just those. Not the stars below.
- [ ] **Pick your 3 system-design lead-ins** from [`02-system-design-rounds.md`](02-system-design-rounds.md). Say each 60-second lead aloud once.

### Practical setup

- [ ] **Test your video + audio** on whatever platform the interview is on. Lighting, microphone, background, internet. Resolve any flakiness now, not 5 minutes before.
- [ ] **Confirm the interview time and time zone.** Set 2 reminders — 1 hour before and 15 minutes before.
- [ ] **Pin `applied-ai-lab`** on your GitHub profile if you haven't. Verify the README renders correctly.
- [ ] **Verify your resume** has the rewritten StrengthLens bullets + the applied-ai-lab project line + the technical-skills edits from [`docs/00-interview-prep/04-resume-bullets.md`](../00-interview-prep/04-resume-bullets.md). Send the same version you submitted to JCI — don't surprise them with a different one.
- [ ] **Have water at the desk.** Not coffee mid-interview, not energy drinks. Water.

### What NOT to do

- ❌ Don't try to learn something new. The marginal new fact won't help; it'll just crowd out things you already know.
- ❌ Don't read the entire 40-question doc again. Diminishing returns.
- ❌ Don't compare your prep to other candidates' (or to imagined ones). You've prepped more than 95% of applicants. Trust that.

### Sleep

Aim for 7+ hours. The single highest-ROI activity the night before a senior interview is sleep. Cognitive performance under pressure is a sleep function. **More prep is not worth less sleep.**

## Morning of

### 60 minutes before

- [ ] Eat something real — protein + complex carbs. Not just coffee.
- [ ] Read the [one-page summary](#one-page-summary) below. Just that. 5 minutes.
- [ ] Open these tabs and leave them open:
  - GitHub repo home: `github.com/XKushal/applied-ai-lab`
  - Capstone README: `github.com/XKushal/applied-ai-lab/tree/main/src/04-capstone`
  - Concepts index: `github.com/XKushal/applied-ai-lab/tree/main/docs/01-concepts`
  - Your resume (PDF, the same one you submitted)
  - The JD (so you can pull a sentence verbatim if needed)
- [ ] If you're doing the docker demo: spin up `docker compose up` so it's ready to screen-share if asked. Test that the chat UI works once.

### 15 minutes before

- [ ] Bathroom.
- [ ] Water at the desk.
- [ ] Phone on silent, **face down**.
- [ ] Close Slack, email, anything that might notify on screen-share.
- [ ] Open the meeting link. Wait there with a notepad app or paper.
- [ ] **Read your pitch one more time.** That's the only thing you should re-read in the final 15 minutes.

### When you join

- [ ] Smile. Say your name. Confirm you can hear them.
- [ ] If they ask "how are you" — say "good, thanks — looking forward to this." Don't dump on the small talk.
- [ ] Have a pen and paper visible. Take notes during the interview (proof of engagement).
- [ ] When you don't know something, **say so.** Bluffing is the single fastest way to lose a senior interview.
- [ ] At the end, **have your 3 questions ready** from [`01-likely-questions.md`](01-likely-questions.md) Section I.

## During the interview — the spoken patterns to keep using

### When asked something open-ended → restate before answering

> *"To make sure I understand — you're asking about [restated]. Is that right?"*

Buys you 5 seconds to think, confirms scope, signals you're not just answering by reflex.

### When asked something you don't know

> *"I don't have direct production experience with that. Here's how I'd think about it…"* and then reason aloud.

### When you finish an answer and they're silent

Don't fill silence. Wait. Senior interviews use silence as a probe; if you fill it, you're working harder than they are.

### When asked about something you DO know deeply

Resist the urge to dump. Give a tight 90-second answer with one specific example. If they want more they'll ask. **Over-explaining is a junior tell.**

## After the interview

### Same day

- [ ] **Send a thank-you note** within 2 hours. Short — 3-5 sentences. Reference one specific thing they said (proof you were listening). Not generic.
- [ ] **Write down everything you remember** while it's fresh:
  - Each question asked
  - Anything you said you'd "follow up on" (you must follow up)
  - Anyone they mentioned by name
  - Anything they hinted about next steps
- [ ] **One short post-mortem note to yourself**: what went well, what you'd say differently. Useful for the next round or the next interview.

### What NOT to do

- ❌ Don't refresh email every 10 minutes for a week. They'll get back to you when they do.
- ❌ Don't share details on LinkedIn before you have an offer in hand.
- ❌ Don't tell yourself a single moment doomed the interview. You don't know what mattered to them; let them tell you.
